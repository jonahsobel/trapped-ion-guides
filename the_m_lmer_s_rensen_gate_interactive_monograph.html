The Mølmer–Sørensen Gate: An Interactive Physical & Mathematical Breakdown

How a symmetric bichromatic laser field turns the shared harmonic motion of two trapped ions into a deterministic, temperature-resilient spin-entangling gate.

1. The Physical Bus

Two trapped ions sit micrometers apart—far too distant for direct magnetic spin-spin coupling. Instead, strong Coulomb repulsion locks their motion into collective normal modes (phonons) that act as a shared quantum data bus.

2. The Thermal Bottleneck

Early schemes (like the 1995 Cirac–Zoller gate) required cooling the motional mode strictly to the ground state ($n=0$) because single-phonon transition rates scale as $\sqrt{n+1}$. Any thermal phonon occupation ruins gate fidelity.

3. The MS Breakthrough

By driving both red and blue motional sidebands simultaneously with detuning $\pm\delta$, virtual phonon pathways interfere destructively in $n$, while transiently tracing a closed loop in motional phase space whose area yields a pure $\sigma_x^{(1)}\sigma_x^{(2)}$ geometric phase.

To understand the Mølmer–Sørensen (MS) gate deeply, one needs to bridge two complementary physical pictures that appear in the literature:

  • Picture A — Two-Photon Virtual Interference (Mølmer & Sørensen, 1999): In the energy-eigenstate basis $|\text{spin}, n\rangle$, the transition $|\downarrow\downarrow, n\rangle \leftrightarrow |\uparrow\uparrow, n\rangle$ proceeds via intermediate states $|n+1\rangle$ and $|n-1\rangle$. Because one pathway is detuned by $-\delta$ with matrix element squared $\propto (n+1)$ and the other by $+\delta$ with matrix element squared $\propto n$, their sum is proportional to $\frac{n+1}{-\delta} + \frac{n}{+\delta} = -\frac{1}{\delta}$. The phonon number $n$ cancels out completely.
  • Picture B — Spin-Dependent Phase-Space Trajectories (Sørensen & Mølmer, 2000): In the collective $S_x = \sigma_x^{(1)} + \sigma_x^{(2)}$ eigenbasis, the bichromatic field acts as an off-resonant harmonic driving force whose direction depends on the collective spin eigenvalue $s_x \in \{-2, 0, +2\}$. The spin states $|++\rangle$ and $|--\rangle$ are pushed around circular loops in $(X, P)$ phase space, returning to the origin at $t_{\text{gate}} = 2\pi/\delta$ with zero residual spin-motion entanglement and an accumulated geometric phase proportional to the enclosed area.
Section 1

Physical Setup & The Bichromatic Laser Drive

Consider two identical atomic ions ($i = 1, 2$) of mass $M$ confined in a linear radio-frequency Paul trap. Each ion encodes a qubit in two long-lived internal electronic levels, $|\downarrow\rangle \equiv |0\rangle$ and $|\uparrow\rangle \equiv |1\rangle$, separated by energy $\hbar\omega_0$.

Because both ions carry positive charge $+e$, their positions along the trap axis are coupled by Coulomb repulsion. Small oscillations around their equilibrium positions decompose into two normal modes:

  1. Center-of-Mass (COM) mode ($\nu_{\text{COM}} = \nu$): Both ions oscillate together in phase with equal amplitude. The normal-mode participation vector is $\mathbf{b}_{\text{COM}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(+1, +1)^T$, meaning both ions share the exact same Lamb–Dicke coupling $\eta_1 = \eta_2 = \eta$.
  2. Stretch (Breathing) mode ($\nu_{\text{str}} = \sqrt{3}\nu$): The two ions oscillate $180^\circ$ out of phase, compressing and stretching their inter-ion distance. Here $\mathbf{b}_{\text{str}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(+1, -1)^T$, so $\eta_1 = -\eta_2$.

To execute an MS gate via the COM mode of frequency $\nu$, we illuminate both ions globally with a single laser beam containing two simultaneous frequency tones (a bichromatic field) symmetrically detuned around the bare carrier transition $\omega_0$:

$$\omega_{\text{blue}} = \omega_0 + (\nu - \delta), \qquad \omega_{\text{red}} = \omega_0 - (\nu - \delta)$$

Notice the symmetry of these two frequencies: the blue tone sits just below the upper motional sideband ($\omega_0 + \nu$) by a small detuning $\delta$, while the red tone sits just above the lower motional sideband ($\omega_0 - \nu$) by $+\delta$. Crucially, their sum always satisfies the two-photon carrier resonance condition:

$$\omega_{\text{blue}} + \omega_{\text{red}} = \big(\omega_0 + \nu - \delta\big) + \big(\omega_0 - \nu + \delta\big) = 2\omega_0$$

Thus, absorbing one photon from the blue tone and one photon from the red tone delivers total energy $2\hbar\omega_0$—the exact energy needed to flip both spins ($|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle \leftrightarrow |\uparrow\uparrow\rangle$) with zero net phonons left in the trap.

Section 2

Step-by-Step Hamiltonian Derivation

Let us derive the Mølmer–Sørensen interaction Hamiltonian from first principles, tracking every approximation and physical factor so there are no hidden steps.

Step 1: Unperturbed Atomic & Motional Hamiltonian ($H_0$)

In the absence of laser fields, the two internal spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ qubits and the shared axial Center-of-Mass (COM) harmonic oscillator mode are described by:

$$H_0 = \frac{\hbar\omega_0}{2} \sum_{i=1}^2 \sigma_z^{(i)} + \hbar\nu \left(a^\dagger a + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

Here $\sigma_z^{(i)} = |\uparrow\rangle_i\langle\uparrow| - |\downarrow\rangle_i\langle\downarrow|$ is the Pauli-$Z$ operator of ion $i$, and $a^\dagger, a$ are the phonon creation and annihilation operators of the COM mode satisfying $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$. Each ion's displacement from equilibrium projected onto the COM mode is $\hat{z}_i = z_0(a + a^\dagger)$, where $z_0 = \sqrt{\hbar / (4M\nu)}$ is the two-ion zero-point spread.

Step 2: Bichromatic Drive & The Lamb–Dicke Expansion

Both ions are illuminated with equal Rabi frequency $\Omega$ by the bichromatic laser field at frequencies $\omega_{\text{blue}} = \omega_0 + (\nu - \delta)$ and $\omega_{\text{red}} = \omega_0 - (\nu - \delta)$ with optical phases $\phi_{\text{blue}}, \phi_{\text{red}}$:

$$H_{\text{drive}}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^2 \frac{\hbar\Omega}{2} \left(\sigma_+^{(i)} + \sigma_-^{(i)}\right) \Big[ \cos\!\left(k\hat{z}_i - \omega_{\text{blue}} t - \phi_{\text{blue}}\right) + \cos\!\left(k\hat{z}_i - \omega_{\text{red}} t - \phi_{\text{red}}\right) \Big]$$

We define the dimensionless Lamb–Dicke parameter $\eta \equiv k z_0 = k\sqrt{\hbar / (4M\nu)}$, which measures the ratio of the motional ground-state wavepacket size $z_0$ to the optical wavelength $\lambda / (2\pi) = 1/k$. In the Lamb–Dicke regime ($\eta\sqrt{2\bar{n}+1} \ll 1$), we Taylor-expand the spatial phase factor to first order in $\eta$:

$$e^{i k \hat{z}_i} = e^{i \eta (a + a^\dagger)} \approx \mathbb{I} + i\eta\left(a + a^\dagger\right) + \mathcal{O}(\eta^2)$$

Step 3: Interaction Picture & Rotating Wave Approximation (RWA)

Transforming into the interaction picture with respect to $H_0$ via $H_I(t) = e^{i H_0 t/\hbar} H_{\text{drive}}(t) e^{-i H_0 t/\hbar}$ substitutes $\sigma_+^{(i)} \to \sigma_+^{(i)} e^{i\omega_0 t}$ and $a \to a e^{-i\nu t}$. Assuming weak driving relative to the trap frequency ($\Omega, \delta \ll \nu \ll \omega_0$), we apply the Rotating Wave Approximation to drop fast-oscillating carrier terms ($\propto e^{\pm i(\nu-\delta)t}$) and far-off-resonant sideband terms ($\propto e^{\pm i(2\nu-\delta)t}$).

Only two near-resonant processes survive: the blue laser tone drives the Blue Sideband (BSB) ($\sigma_+^{(i)} a^\dagger e^{i\delta t}$) and the red laser tone drives the Red Sideband (RSB) ($\sigma_+^{(i)} a e^{-i\delta t}$):

$$H_I(t) = \underbrace{\frac{\hbar\eta\Omega}{2} \sum_{i=1}^2 \left( i e^{-i\phi_{\text{blue}}} \sigma_+^{(i)} a^\dagger e^{i\delta t} + \text{h.c.} \right)}_{H_{\text{BSB}}(t) \text{ (creates phonon + flips spin)}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{\hbar\eta\Omega}{2} \sum_{i=1}^2 \left( i e^{-i\phi_{\text{red}}} \sigma_+^{(i)} a e^{-i\delta t} + \text{h.c.} \right)}_{H_{\text{RSB}}(t) \text{ (annihilates phonon + flips spin)}}$$

Step 4: Factoring into the Mølmer–Sørensen Hamiltonian

Define the spin phase $\phi_s \equiv \frac{\phi_{\text{blue}} + \phi_{\text{red}}}{2} - \frac{\pi}{2}$ and the motional phase $\phi_m \equiv \frac{\phi_{\text{blue}} - \phi_{\text{red}}}{2}$. Because the blue and red tones have equal amplitude $\eta\Omega$, the sum $H_{\text{BSB}}(t) + H_{\text{RSB}}(t)$ factors cleanly into a tensor product of a collective spin operator and a harmonically modulated motional operator. Setting $\phi_s = 0$ and $\phi_m = 0$ yields the canonical Mølmer–Sørensen Hamiltonian:

The Two-Photon Interference Picture: Why Phonon Number $n$ Cancels Out

Before solving the time evolution at arbitrary gate speed, let us examine Mølmer and Sørensen's original 1999 insight in the weak-drive regime ($\eta\Omega \ll \delta$). Suppose the ions start in $|\downarrow\downarrow, n\rangle$, where the motional mode contains $n$ phonons. How does the bichromatic field couple $|\downarrow\downarrow, n\rangle$ to $|\uparrow\uparrow, n\rangle$?

Because $H_{\text{MS}}(t)$ always changes the phonon number by $\Delta n = \pm 1$ while flipping a single spin, no first-order transition exists between $|\downarrow\downarrow, n\rangle$ and $|\uparrow\uparrow, n\rangle$. Instead, the transition occurs at second order in perturbation theory via four virtual two-photon pathways through intermediate states where one ion has flipped ($m \in \{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle, |\downarrow\uparrow\rangle\}$):

Pathway 1: Blue-First (via $|n+1\rangle$)

First, Ion 1 (or 2) absorbs a blue-tone photon ($\omega_{\text{blue}} = \omega_0 + \nu - \delta$), flipping $|\downarrow\rangle \to |\uparrow\rangle$ and creating a virtual phonon $|n\rangle \to |n+1\rangle$. Next, the other ion absorbs a red-tone photon, flipping its spin and annihilating the extra phonon $|n+1\rangle \to |n\rangle$.

$$\text{Matrix element product: } \left(\frac{\hbar\eta\Omega}{2}\sqrt{n+1}\right)^2$$ $$\text{Intermediate energy defect: } E_i - E_{\text{virt}} = -\hbar\delta$$
Pathway 2: Red-First (via $|n-1\rangle$)

First, Ion 1 (or 2) absorbs a red-tone photon ($\omega_{\text{red}} = \omega_0 - \nu + \delta$), flipping $|\downarrow\rangle \to |\uparrow\rangle$ and annihilating a phonon $|n\rangle \to |n-1\rangle$. Next, the other ion absorbs a blue-tone photon, flipping its spin and re-creating the phonon $|n-1\rangle \to |n\rangle$.

$$\text{Matrix element product: } \left(\frac{\hbar\eta\Omega}{2}\sqrt{n}\right)^2$$ $$\text{Intermediate energy defect: } E_i - E_{\text{virt}} = +\hbar\delta$$

Summing the second-order transition amplitudes over both intermediate phonon manifolds ($|n+1\rangle$ and $|n-1\rangle$) and both ion orderings ($|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$ and $|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle$, which gives a factor of $2$), the effective two-photon Rabi coupling $\hbar\Omega_{\text{eff}}$ between $|\downarrow\downarrow, n\rangle$ and $|\uparrow\uparrow, n\rangle$ is:

$$\hbar\Omega_{\text{eff}} = \sum_{m \in \{\uparrow\downarrow, \downarrow\uparrow\}} \left[ \frac{\langle \uparrow\uparrow, n | H_{\text{RSB}} | m, n+1\rangle \langle m, n+1 | H_{\text{BSB}} | \downarrow\downarrow, n\rangle}{-\hbar\delta} + \frac{\langle \uparrow\uparrow, n | H_{\text{BSB}} | m, n-1\rangle \langle m, n-1 | H_{\text{RSB}} | \downarrow\downarrow, n\rangle}{+\hbar\delta} \right]$$ $$\implies \Omega_{\text{eff}} = 2 \left(\frac{\eta\Omega}{2}\right)^2 \left( \underbrace{\frac{n+1}{-\delta}}_{\text{Blue-first}} + \underbrace{\frac{n}{+\delta}}_{\text{Red-first}} \right) = -\frac{(\eta\Omega)^2}{2\delta}\Big((n+1) - n\Big) = -\frac{(\eta\Omega)^2}{2\delta}$$
Why This Cancellation Is Miraculous

Individual single-phonon transitions scale as $\sqrt{n+1}$ or $\sqrt{n}$, meaning a thermally excited ion crystal oscillates at different Rabi frequencies for every Fock state $|n\rangle$. Here, because the blue-first pathway is detuned below $|n+1\rangle$ by $-\delta$ while the red-first pathway is detuned above $|n-1\rangle$ by $+\delta$, their $n$-dependent parts $\mp n/\delta$ have opposite signs and destructively interfere! Only the vacuum commutator term $[a, a^\dagger] = (n+1) - n = 1$ survives, rendering $\Omega_{\text{eff}}$ completely independent of $n$.

Section 4 • Picture B (Exact Solution at Arbitrary Speed)

Phase-Space Trajectories & Geometric Phase

What happens when we increase the laser power so that $\eta\Omega$ is comparable to $\delta$ (the fast-gate regime used in every modern trapped-ion quantum computer)? Now the intermediate phonon states are strongly populated during the gate, and perturbation theory no longer applies.

Remarkably, Sørensen and Mølmer (2000) showed that $H_{\text{MS}}(t)$ admits an exact analytical solution at arbitrary drive strength via the Magnus expansion. Because the commutator of the Hamiltonian at two different times is proportional to $[a, a^\dagger] = \mathbb{I}$:

$$\big[H_{\text{MS}}(t_1),\, H_{\text{MS}}(t_2)\big] = -2i \left(\frac{\hbar\eta\Omega}{2}\right)^2 S_x^2 \sin\!\big(\delta(t_1 - t_2)\big)$$

all third- and higher-order nested commutators in the Magnus expansion vanish identically! The exact time-evolution operator $U(t)$ factors into a spin-dependent phase-space displacement $\hat{D}\big(\alpha(t)S_x\big) = \exp\!\big[(\alpha(t)a^\dagger - \alpha^*(t)a)S_x\big]$ and a pure two-qubit spin-spin phase gate:

$$U(t) = \hat{D}\!\left(\alpha(t) S_x\right) \exp\!\left(-i \Phi(t) S_x^2\right)$$ $$\alpha(t) = \frac{\eta\Omega}{2\delta}\left(e^{-i\delta t} - 1\right), \qquad \Phi(t) = \left(\frac{\eta\Omega}{2\delta}\right)^2 \big(\delta t - \sin(\delta t)\big)$$

What Happens in the $S_x = \sigma_x^{(1)} + \sigma_x^{(2)}$ Eigenbasis?

Let $|\pm\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\downarrow\rangle \pm |\uparrow\rangle)$ denote the eigenstates of $\sigma_x$ with eigenvalues $\pm 1$. The two-qubit collective operator $S_x = \sigma_x^{(1)} + \sigma_x^{(2)}$ has four eigenstates with three distinct eigenvalues $s_x$:

  • $|++\rangle$ ($s_x = +2$): Experiences a strong periodic driving force, tracing a circle $\alpha_{++}(t) = +2\alpha(t)$ of radius $R = \eta\Omega/\delta$ centered at $(-\eta\Omega/\delta, 0)$ in motional $(X, P)$ phase space, while accumulating geometric phase $e^{-i 4\Phi(t)}$.
  • $|--\rangle$ ($s_x = -2$): Experiences the equal and opposite force, tracing a circle $\alpha_{--}(t) = -2\alpha(t)$ on the opposite side of the origin and accumulating the same geometric phase $e^{-i 4\Phi(t)}$ (since $(-2)^2 = +4$).
  • $|+-\rangle$ and $|-+\rangle$ ($s_x = 0$): The forces on Ion 1 and Ion 2 cancel out! Their motional state remains completely stationary at the origin ($\alpha = 0$) and accumulates zero geometric phase ($s_x^2 = 0$).

Gate Closure & The $XX(\pi/4)$ Entangling Condition

Note that $S_x^2 = (\sigma_x^{(1)} + \sigma_x^{(2)})^2 = 2\mathbb{I} + 2\sigma_x^{(1)}\sigma_x^{(2)}$. For the gate to leave the spins completely disentangled from the motional mode, two conditions must be met at the gate completion time $t = t_{\text{gate}}$:

  1. Loop Closure ($\alpha(t_{\text{gate}}) = 0$): The circular trajectory must return to the origin after an integer number of loops $K \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$, requiring $\delta t_{\text{gate}} = 2\pi K$.
  2. Maximal Entanglement ($4\Phi(t_{\text{gate}}) = \pi/2$): The differential geometric phase between the $s_x = \pm 2$ manifold and the $s_x = 0$ manifold must equal $\pi/2$ (equivalent to $\exp(-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_x^{(1)}\sigma_x^{(2)})$ up to global phase). Setting $\Phi(t_{\text{gate}}) = \left(\frac{\eta\Omega}{2\delta}\right)^2 (2\pi K) = \frac{\pi}{8}$ yields the famous Mølmer–Sørensen detuning condition:
    $$\delta = 2\sqrt{K}\,\eta\Omega \qquad \xrightarrow{\text{For } K=1 \text{ loop}} \qquad \delta = 2\eta\Omega, \quad t_{\text{gate}} = \frac{2\pi}{\delta} = \frac{\pi}{\eta\Omega}$$

Applying $U(t_{\text{gate}}) = e^{-i\pi/4}\exp\!\left(-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_x^{(1)}\sigma_x^{(2)}\right)$ to the initial computational state $|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\big(|++\rangle + |--\rangle - |+-\rangle - |-+\rangle\big)$ produces the maximally entangled Bell state:

$$|\Psi(t_{\text{gate}})\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle - i|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle\Big)$$
